# Writing Case Studies

A case study proves how you think, not what you made: lead with the outcome, then show the hard decisions, the evidence behind them, and what you'd do differently.

## Choose the workflow

- **Pick which project(s) to feature** → "Step 1" below.
- **Write a portfolio case study from notes or a project** → "Write workflow" + full template in [reference/case-study-templates.md](reference/case-study-templates.md).
- **Critique or shorten an existing case study** → "Review workflow" + rubric in [reference/writing-and-review.md](reference/writing-and-review.md).
- **Portfolio review presentation (45 min)** → slide outline in [reference/case-study-templates.md](reference/case-study-templates.md).
- **Headlines, impact statements, no-metrics problem** → [reference/writing-and-review.md](reference/writing-and-review.md).
- **Interview prep / likely questions** → [reference/writing-and-review.md](reference/writing-and-review.md).
- **Internal case study or project retrospective** → "Internal variant" below + template in [reference/case-study-templates.md](reference/case-study-templates.md).

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Outcome first.** The hero/TL;DR states problem, your role, and result in ≤ 3 lines; a hiring manager decides in 30-60 seconds.
2. **"I" for your work, "we" for the team** - and say exactly what you owned vs. contributed. Never claim the team's work as yours.
3. **Show 2-4 key decisions with trade-offs**: options considered, why you chose one, what you gave up, who disagreed. Decisions beat deliverable galleries.
4. **Every claim has evidence**: research finding, data, quote, test result, or stakeholder outcome.
5. **Quantify impact honestly.** Real metrics first; otherwise proxy or leading metrics, labeled as such ("pilot", "estimated", "in usability tests"). Never invent or round up numbers.
6. **Name the constraints** (timeline, legacy system, compliance, design system, team size, permissions model) - they explain your choices.
7. **Make enterprise complexity legible**: name the personas (end user, admin, buyer, IT/security), stakeholders, and systems; use one simple diagram instead of paragraphs.
8. **Before/after visuals with captions** that state the insight, not the screen name ("Grouping approvals by risk cut review time" not "Dashboard v2").
9. **Respect NDA**: anonymize the client, abstract or redraw visuals, remove real data and names, use relative metrics; get permission when in doubt.
10. **Reflect genuinely**: what didn't work, what you'd change, what you learned - one specific lesson beats three generic ones.
11. **Length fits the audience**: portfolio page 800-1,500 words and 8-15 visuals; skim layer readable in under 60 seconds; deck 15-25 slides for 45 minutes.
12. **Accessible artifact**: real text (not text baked into images), alt text on visuals, sufficient contrast, descriptive headings.

## Write workflow

Copy this checklist and track progress:

```
Case study checklist:
- [ ] 1. Pick the project and the audience (hiring manager, design leader, internal)
- [ ] 2. Gather raw material: brief, role, timeline, research, iterations, metrics, quotes
- [ ] 3. Write the one-sentence story (CAR) and the hero/TL;DR
- [ ] 4. Choose 2-4 key decisions and their evidence and trade-offs
- [ ] 5. Draft sections with the full template; add visuals with insight captions
- [ ] 6. Write impact (real or labeled proxy metrics) and reflection
- [ ] 7. Apply NDA pass and accessibility pass
- [ ] 8. Run the quality check; revise until it passes
- [ ] 9. Produce the skim layer and (if needed) deck outline + interview Q&A
```

**Step 1 - Pick the project.** Score candidates 1-3 on: relevance to the target role, your ownership level, complexity (multi-persona, constraints, systems), evidence of impact, and story (a real tension or pivot). Feature 2-4 projects; the strongest goes first. A shipped project with modest impact and clear decisions beats a polished concept with no constraints.

**Step 3 - One-sentence story.** CAR: "Faced with [challenge], I [action/key decision], which led to [result]." For interviews, expand to STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). If you can't write this sentence, you don't have the story yet - ask for missing pieces.

**Step 4 - Key decisions.** For each: the question, the options, the evidence, the call, the trade-off, the outcome. This is what design leaders hire for.

**Step 6 - No data?** Use proxies: usability test task success and time on task, SUS/SEQ deltas, support ticket volume or categories, adoption or rollout stats, stakeholder decisions enabled, design system reuse, sales/CS feedback, qualitative quotes. See impact formulas in [reference/writing-and-review.md](reference/writing-and-review.md).

## Review workflow

1. Do a 60-second skim test: can you state the problem, role, and outcome? If not, that is finding #1.
2. Score with the rubric in [reference/writing-and-review.md](reference/writing-and-review.md) (each criterion 1-4).
3. List the top 5 fixes, most impactful first, with a rewritten example for each.
4. Suggest cuts (process theater, every-artifact galleries, tool lists) with a target word count.

## Internal variant (team case study / retrospective)

Audience is stakeholders and peers, not hiring managers. Shift emphasis: shared ownership ("we"), business outcome, what we learned, what we'll repeat or change, and decisions or asks for leadership. Add a timeline, a "what went well / what didn't / what we'll change" table, and reusable assets (patterns, research, components). Blameless tone; name process issues, not people.

## Quality check (run before delivering, revise until all pass)

- [ ] Hero states problem, role, and outcome in ≤ 3 lines; skim layer works in 60 seconds
- [ ] Role and ownership are explicit; "I" vs. "we" is accurate
- [ ] 2-4 decisions show options, evidence, trade-off, and outcome
- [ ] Constraints and enterprise context (personas, stakeholders, systems) are named
- [ ] Impact is quantified or proxies are clearly labeled; nothing invented
- [ ] Every visual has an insight caption and alt text; before/after included
- [ ] NDA pass done (names, data, logos, screenshots)
- [ ] Reflection names a specific lesson and what you'd do differently
- [ ] Length within target; no process theater or tool lists

## Output template

```markdown
# [Outcome-led headline]
**TL;DR:** [Problem] → [my role + key action] → [result with metric or labeled proxy]
**Role:** [title, what I owned] · **Team:** [roles] · **Timeline:** [duration] · **Users:** [personas]

## Context
## The problem  (who, pain, evidence, business stakes)
## Constraints
## Process and key decisions
### Decision 1: [question] - options, evidence, call, trade-off
## Iterations  (before/after with captions)
## Solution
## Impact  (metrics; proxies labeled)
## What I learned / what I'd do differently

---
Visual list: [#, what it shows, insight caption, alt text, NDA treatment]
Open gaps: [missing data or permissions to confirm]
```

## Example

**Input:** "I redesigned the access request flow in our admin console. Took 4 months, worked with 1 PM and 3 engineers. Not sure of the numbers but support said tickets went down a lot."

**Output (hero and impact excerpt):**
> **Cutting access-request tickets by redesigning approvals for IT admins**
> **TL;DR:** IT admins at 200+ customer accounts handled access requests by email and spreadsheets. As lead designer, I reframed the flow around risk-based approval queues, which reduced access-related support tickets *(confirm the % with support data)* and was adopted by [X] accounts in the first quarter.
> **Role:** Lead product designer - research, flows, UI, usability testing · **Team:** 1 PM, 3 engineers · **Timeline:** 4 months

Gaps flagged: exact ticket reduction (ask support for before/after counts over equal periods), adoption count, usability-test task success, one admin quote.
