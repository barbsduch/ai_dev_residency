## Headline formulas
- **[Verb]-ing [outcome] for [persona]** - "Cutting onboarding time in half for HR admins"
- **How [I/we] [achieved outcome] by [key move]** - "How we reduced approval delays by grouping requests by risk"
- **From [old state] to [new state]** - "From spreadsheets to self-serve: rebuilding access reviews"
- **[Persona] [did new behavior]** - "IT admins finally trusted bulk import"

Avoid: project codenames, "Redesign of the dashboard", "App X case study".

## Impact statement formulas
- **Metric:** "[Metric] went from [baseline] to [after] over [timeframe], measured by [source]."
- **Business:** "[Change] contributed to [business outcome: renewal, deal, reduced cost], according to [stakeholder/source]."
- **Proxy (no live data):** "In usability testing with [n] [persona], task success rose from [x/n] to [y/n] and average time fell from [a] to [b]."
- **Qualitative + scale:** "Adopted by [n] teams / [n] products in the design system within [time]; [quote]."
- **Enablement:** "The research reframed the roadmap: leadership moved [feature] from Q4 to Q2."

Attribution language: "contributed to", "alongside a pricing change", "pilot with 4 accounts" - honest attribution is more credible than inflated claims.

## Metrics menu (real and proxy)

| Category | Real metrics | Proxy / leading metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Efficiency | Task time, steps, clicks in production | Time on task in usability tests |
| Effectiveness | Completion/error rate, rework | Task success in tests, SEQ (1-7) |
| Satisfaction | NPS, CSAT, SUS in product | SUS in tests (68 = average), quotes |
| Adoption | Feature adoption %, active accounts, retention | Pilot sign-ups, beta opt-in |
| Support | Ticket volume/category, handle time | CS team estimate, top-ticket coverage |
| Revenue | Conversion, expansion, deals unblocked | Sales feedback, RFP requirements met |
| Org / system | Design system reuse, dev time saved | Components adopted, teams onboarded |
| Compliance | WCAG AA issues resolved, audit passed | Accessibility audit score change |

If no data at all: describe what you would measure, the baseline you'd capture, and the signal you saw.

## STAR and CAR framing
- **CAR** (case study spine): Challenge → Action → Result. Use for the TL;DR and each decision.
- **STAR** (interviews): Situation (context, 15%) → Task (your responsibility, 10%) → Action (what *you* did and why, 50%) → Result (outcome + learning, 25%).
- Add **trade-off** and **reflection** to either: "...and if I did it again, I'd..."

## Before/after rewrites

| Before (weak) | After (strong) |
|---|---|
| "I conducted user research, created personas, made wireframes, and did usability testing." | "Eight admin interviews showed approvals stalled because risk wasn't visible, so I designed a queue sorted by risk and tested it with 5 admins - all 5 found high-risk requests first." |
| "The new dashboard was a huge success." | "Weekly active admins rose from 31% to 52% in the first quarter after launch (product analytics)." |
| "We worked closely with stakeholders." | "Legal wanted every approval logged with a reason; I proposed preset reasons plus free text, which met audit needs without slowing admins." |
| "Final designs" (gallery of 12 screens) | 3 annotated screens: primary flow, error recovery, no-permission state - each with an insight caption |
| "I learned a lot about collaboration." | "I'd bring engineering into concept testing - two directions turned out infeasible after we'd tested them." |

## Audience tailoring

| Audience | Time | Wants | Emphasize |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter / hiring manager (skim) | 30-60 s | Relevance, level, outcome | Headline, TL;DR, role, 3 numbers, hero visual |
| Design leadership / panel | 5-10 min read, 45 min review | Judgment, craft, influence | Decisions, trade-offs, stakeholder moments, reflection |
| Internal stakeholders | 10-min readout | Business result, what's next | Goals vs. results, decisions, asks, reusable assets |
| Design manager role | - | Team and org impact | Team setup, coaching, process change, cross-team adoption |

For senior/manager roles: show scope (multiple teams, roadmap influence), how you enabled others, and organizational outcomes - not only screens.

## Common mistakes
- Process theater: every method listed, none tied to a decision.
- Deliverable galleries with no captions or rationale.
- Hiding the outcome at the bottom.
- "We" everywhere - reader can't tell what you did.
- No constraints, so decisions look arbitrary.
- Invented or unsourced metrics; or no impact section at all.
- Too long (3,000+ words) or too many projects (6+ weak ones).
- Only the happy path; no error, empty, or permission states.
- Text baked into images; no alt text.
- Generic reflection ("communication is key").

## Self-review rubric
Score 1 (missing) - 2 (weak) - 3 (solid) - 4 (excellent). Ship at ≥ 3 on every criterion; fix any 1-2 first.

| Criterion | 4 looks like |
|---|---|
| Skimmability | Problem, role, outcome clear in 60 s from headline, TL;DR, headings, captions |
| Problem framing | Specific persona, evidence, and business cost |
| Role clarity | Exact ownership; "I" vs. "we" accurate |
| Decision-making | 2-4 decisions with options, evidence, trade-off |
| Enterprise context | Personas, stakeholders, constraints, systems legible (with a diagram) |
| Evidence | Research and data back each key claim |
| Impact | Quantified with source and timeframe, or labeled proxies |
| Craft | Before/after and edge states; annotated, high-quality visuals |
| Reflection | Specific lesson and "what I'd change" |
| Length & accessibility | Within target length; real text, alt text, contrast |

## Interview questions and strong answer structure
Answer structure: **headline answer (1 sentence) → context → what I did and why → result → reflection**. Aim for 1-2 minutes.

Likely questions (prepare one example for each):
1. Walk me through your role - what did you own vs. the team?
2. What was the hardest decision, and what did you trade off?
3. What did the research change about your direction?
4. Tell me about a disagreement with a PM or engineer. How was it resolved?
5. How did you measure success? What would you measure now?
6. What would you do differently?
7. How did you handle multiple personas (admin vs. end user)?
8. What constraints shaped the solution most?
9. How did you work within (or extend) the design system?
10. How did you ensure accessibility?
11. What didn't ship, and why?
12. (Manager roles) How did you set up the team, grow designers, or change process?

Example strong answer (Q2): "The hardest call was capping preview at 5,000 rows. Engineering could only validate synchronously up to that size, and 95% of imports were smaller. I proposed preview for most customers and an async email report for the largest, and tested both with admins. Adoption rose to 47%; the trade-off was a slower path for our biggest accounts, which we're now addressing with a streaming validator."
